# Beat Factory — Art Pipeline (v3: visual identity)

All current art is procedural and original. It is generated by `Tools > Beat Factory > Art > Regenerate Art` (SdfPainter, code in `Scripts/Editor/Art/`).
Every PNG is a **slot**. Replace it with final art of the **same name, size and pivot** (transparent PNG unless noted), then run `Tools > Beat Factory > Build Game Scene`.
Scene builds never overwrite existing files; only the Regenerate menu does.

## Art direction (paste at the start of EVERY prompt)
"Premium stylized 2D illustration for a modern mobile music-production game. Clean painterly vector look with soft cel shading, rim light from a cool monitor glow (cyan/violet) and a warm key light. Realistic proportions, slightly stylized (heads about 1/6.5 of the body). Detailed faces, hands, hair and fabric folds. Clean silhouettes, no outlines or thin dark line art. NOT pixel art, NOT chibi, NOT flat vector, NOT photo. Original designs only: no real people, brands, logos, album covers or artist names. Palette: deep charcoal-violet base #0D0B1A / #17132B, neon pink #FF3DC8, cyan #3DE1FF, mint #4DFFA8, orange #FF8A3D, gold #F2C14E."

Keep one tool, one seed family and one style reference image for the whole set, so every character and room matches.

## DAW monitor — `Art/DAW/daw_background.png` (400×250) + runtime UI (`DawScreen`)
The fictional DAW "BEATLAB" is drawn as Unity UI, NOT a static picture. The parts are:
- transport with play LED, BEATLAB logo, time and BPM;
- channel rack with track names (KICK, 808, SNARE, HATS, KEYS, VOX);
- a timeline of colored clips with a moving playhead;
- a mixer (6 meters and faders);
- a piano roll with notes and its own playhead;
- 4 rotating synth knobs;
- a waveform.

When idle, the meters, waveform, faders and knobs drift. On MAKE BEAT a clip pops in with a white flash, new piano-roll notes appear, the transport runs, the meters jump and the waveform swells. Producers' laptops always play and react to their beats. On a studio upgrade the screen boots: black → logo → interface.
Background prompt (static chrome only): "Original dark music production software interface skin, 400x250, transport bar on top, empty channel rack on the left, empty timeline grid, bottom panel with empty mixer strips, piano-roll keys and grid, four synth knob bases and a waveform box; deep violet/navy, thin purple grid lines; NO clips, NO text, NOT FL Studio, no logos."

## Animation (code)
- `CharacterRig`:
  - idle nods at 85 BPM and blinks;
  - Producing = calm typing with the focus face;
  - MAKE BEAT = fast typing, excited face, strong head bob;
  - Success = jump, arms up, smile;
  - Upgrade = proud chin-up pulse.
- The player's outfit swaps per studio tier (`StudioSceneView.playerBodies/playerArms`).
- Studio upgrade show:
  - lights go out, the new room appears;
  - lights flicker on;
  - equipment pops in;
  - DAW screens boot;
  - the character is proud, then celebrates;
  - confetti.

## Concerts: performers + crowd (generated, `Art/Characters/Artists/Stage`, `Art/Venues/crowd_atlas.png`)
- **Single source of truth:** every artist is defined once (`ArtGenerator.Roster` → `Look`). The portrait, the posters, the LED-screen image and the full-body stage performer (heads per expression at 2×, torso, arm, leg) are all drawn from that same `Look`. The builder copies the description and the stage sprites into `ArtistData.visual` (`ArtistVisualIdentity`).
- **Stage performer** (`PerformerRig`): a layered rig with these states: walk-in, rap (mic at the mouth, the mouth moves), hype, walk, crowd interaction at the stage edge, point, big drop jump, and end of show (wave + bow).
- **Crowd** (`ConcertCrowd` + `CrowdLayer`): white atlas parts tinted per fan (skin, hair, clothes) and drawn as 3 meshes (front row · far blobs (LOD) · detailed fans), with no GameObject per fan.
  - Visible fans grow with popularity: about 50 → 200 → 600 → 1,400 → 3,500. The number is reduced for half-empty houses and capped per venue.
  - Fan moves: idle, hands up, jump, dance, sing, phone, clap, hype, point and reach (front row). Fans pick a move by the music's intensity and their own excitement.
  - Reactions: a wave that spreads from the artist, a surge on the drop, phone-light waves, signs in the front row, security from 50% popularity and photographers' flashes in the pit.
- **Director** (`ConcertStageView`): song sections normal → build-up → drop → chorus → big moment → end. Camera shots: wide, stage, artist close-up, crowd, big drop.

## Concert light show + music
- `StageLightShow`: moving heads, laser patterns (fan · sweep L→R / R→L · X-cross · frame the artist · over the crowd), follow spots + backlight on the artist, floor uplights, LED panels, haze, CO2 smoke, flame bursts (arena+; left → right 0.2–0.4 s apart), spark gerbs/waterfall, capped flashes. Sprites: `fx_laser_core/glow`, `fx_flame`, `fx_spark` (generated).
- Draw order keeps the artist readable: back lights/lasers → ARTIST → wing pyro/front lasers → crowd. Flash alpha is capped at 0.15.
- Quality LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH (Settings → Concert Effects, AUTO by device) scales lasers, movers, sparks, haze and crowd size.
- Music (`ArtistConcertMusic` + `ConcertTrackSynth`): an original soundtrack per artist, rendered on a worker thread. The genre sets the base sound; the artist's id and look add the flavour (dark / bright / minimal). Arrangement: INTRO → VERSE → BUILD-UP → DROP → CHORUS → BIG MOMENT → OUTRO. Visuals follow its beat/kick markers. Settings → Concert Music (on/off + volume); the game music is ducked during the show. Samples: `ConcertMusicSamples/*.wav` (project root).
